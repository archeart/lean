\section{Planned lower-bound argument}
The following are informal proof obligations, not Lean axioms or checked
theorems. Here $J(\Pi)=I((X,Y);T\mid D,R)$ denotes the intended conditional
information cost under the switch distribution in the mathematical plan.
That protocol information cost has not yet been defined in Lean.

\begin{lemma}[Communication information bound: unproved]
\label{goal:upper}
\uses{thm:support,thm:entropy,def:cost}
For the intended input distribution,
$J(\Pi)\leq \operatorname{cost}(\Pi)\log 2$.
Public randomness is conditioned on and must not be charged as communication.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Linear information bound: unproved]
\label{goal:lower}
\uses{thm:factorization,thm:entropy,def:correct,def:disj}
There is an absolute $\kappa>0$ such that every protocol computing DISJ with
pointwise error at most $1/3$ satisfies $J(\Pi)\geq\kappa n$.
The missing steps include the switch distribution, Hellinger inequalities,
the AND bound, coordinate decomposition, and actual coordinate embeddings.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-model DISJ lower bound: unproved]
\label{goal:target}
\lean{Disjointness.FiniteLowerBoundClaim}
\uses{goal:upper,goal:lower}
There exist absolute $a>0$ and $n_0$ such that, for every $n\geq n_0$,
every protocol in the finite seed tree model computing DISJ with pointwise
error at most $1/3$ has communication cost at least $an$.
The linked Lean declaration only defines this target proposition; it does
not prove it. Coverage of conventional public-coin protocols additionally
requires the normalization and output-overhead bridges in the audit.
\end{theorem}
